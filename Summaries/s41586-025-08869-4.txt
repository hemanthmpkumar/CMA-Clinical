Beyond measuring standalone performance, we integrated this model into an interactive interface to measure how well AMIE could assist clinicians in developing a DDx. Using a set of challenging real-world case reports from the New England Journal of Medicine (NEJM) clinicopatho- logical conferences (CPCs), we compared clinicians’ ability to form a DDx with the assistance of AMIE versus with access to traditional information retrieval tools (such as internet searches and books). AMIE achieved impressive performance in both generating DDx lists that contained the correct diagnosis (top-10 accuracy) and in identifying the correct final diagnosis as the most likely in the list (top-1 accuracy). Under auto- mated model-based evaluation, the quality and the accuracy of the DDx list produced by AMIE was found to be significantly better than the state-of-the-art GPT-4 model available at the time of the experiments1. Perhaps more importantly, AMIE also improved the diagnostic capa- bility of clinicians as measured by the quality of their DDx lists for the evaluated cases.
