## Extracted Abstract / Overview
The application of artificial intelligence (AI) in medical imaging has seen remarkable advances in recent years, particularly through the use of deep learning models trained on large-scale datasets. However, despite impressive performance on in-distribution data, such models often struggle to maintain reliability and accuracy when deployed in new clinical environments—a phe- nomenon commonly attributed to domain shift. Domain adaptation (DA) and domain generalization (DG) have thus emerged as critical areas of re- search, aiming to improve model robustness across diverse imaging modali- ties, scanners, patient populations, and institutions. At the same time, the rise of foundation models—large-scale, pre-trained architectures capable of zero-shot or few-shot learning across tasks and domains—has opened up new possibilities for tackling these challenges with unprecedented flexibility and scale. This survey provides an in-depth exploration of the intersec- tion between DA/DG methods and foundation models in medical imag- ing. We begin by outlining the theoretical motivations for domain-robust learning and describe the unique characteristics of medical imaging that intensify the need for generalization, such as high inter-institutional vari- ability, rare disease prevalence, and modality-specific artifacts. We then review foundational techniques for DA and DG, including supervised and unsupervised strategies, self-supervised learning, adversarial alignment, and meta-learning approaches. Building on this foundation, we analyze how recent advancements in foundation models—particularly vision transform- ers, contrastive pretraining, and vision-language models—are reshaping the landscape of domain-robust AI in medicine.
