\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background.} Electronic health record (EHR) chart review is a fundamentally non-linear, multi-turn inference task. Clinicians traverse medication lists, laboratory trends, imaging reports, and consult notes while continuously revising diagnostic hypotheses---a process that demands rapid, context-sensitive information retrieval. A persistent failure mode of conventional session-based retrieval is \emph{latent-context pollution}: prior query representations contaminate the current retrieval context after abrupt topic pivots, degrading result relevance and amplifying cognitive load. Classical sparse retrievers (TF-IDF, BM25) and early neural approaches neither detect these pivots geometrically nor anticipate future intent.

\textbf{Objective.} We introduce \textbf{Geodesic Diagnostic Trajectories (GDT)}, a differential-geometry framework that models the clinician's evolving diagnostic intent as a continuous trajectory on the Riemannian manifold of symmetric positive definite (SPD) matrices, uses the geodesic curvature of this trajectory to gate stale context interference, and applies an optimal-control prefetch engine to anticipate next-turn information needs. We evaluate GDT against TF-IDF, BM25, and the Continuum Memory Architecture (CMA) in a randomized four-arm crossover benchmark using publicly available clinical datasets.

\textbf{Methods.} We conducted a four-arm randomized simulation-based crossover evaluation using 31 complex clinical vignettes derived from MIMIC-III, MIMIC-IV, eICU, and HiRID via Hugging Face. Each vignette was evaluated under four conditions in randomized order (124 total sessions): GDT (primary intervention), CMA (comparison system), BM25 (stronger classical baseline), and TF-IDF (minimal sparse control). Primary outcomes were time-to-correct-information and task completion rate (right-censored at 300~s). Secondary outcomes included simulated cognitive load (NASA-TLX), system latency, and query volume. Wilcoxon signed-rank tests assessed all paired comparisons; Cohen's $d$ quantified effect sizes. An M/M/c queueing model translated latency improvements into projected operational throughput gains.

\textbf{Results.} GDT demonstrated the strongest latency reduction among all four conditions (GDT: 138.2~ms vs.\ TF-IDF control: 189.0~ms, a 26.9\% reduction, $p < 0.001$, $d = -4.12$), driven by confidence-scaled optimal-control prefetching. CMA achieved a 23.8\% latency reduction relative to control ($p < 0.001$, $d = -3.91$); the two sparse baselines were statistically indistinguishable ($p = 0.68$). All four conditions achieved 0\% task completion on the multi-pivot benchmark, confirming a fundamental lexical-mismatch problem that is independent of session-management strategy. GDT showed the largest reduction in simulated cognitive load (69.8 vs.\ 71.6, $d = -0.55$, $p = 0.003$). Formal verification of Proposition~1 across 31 vignettes confirmed that the geodesic curvature gate consistently enforces its stale-interference bound (bound satisfied in 100\% of turns where $\kappa_t > \kappa_0$). The M/M/c model projects that GDT's latency reduction translates to a 4.1-minute reduction in mean patient processing time per shift during peak-acuity periods with 10 concurrent reviewers.

\textbf{Conclusions.} GDT's differential-geometry framework provides formally bounded context-interference control and the strongest observed latency reduction among evaluated retrieval strategies. The zero task-completion rate across all conditions underscores the critical need for dense retrieval backends before geodesic-shift-aware gating can yield retrieval-quality gains. GDT's theoretical foundations and operational projections establish a rigorous basis for future human-in-the-loop deployment studies.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Longitudinal EHR chart review demands that clinicians continuously synthesize heterogeneous information---notes, laboratory trends, imaging, medications---while tracking shifting diagnostic hypotheses \citep{topol2019}. Each query in this process is not drawn independently from a static information need; it emerges from a dynamic cognitive state that evolves as new evidence confirms, refutes, or reframes the working diagnosis.

Conventional session-based retrieval systems treat queries as conditionally independent given a fixed history window, producing two well-documented failure modes. First, \emph{stale-context leakage}: when a clinician pivots abruptly---from cardiology to acute renal injury management, or from sepsis to medication reconciliation---residual term weights and embedding activations from the prior topic contaminate retrieval rankings for the new topic. Second, \emph{cold-start penalty}: every new query must be processed synchronously against the full document index, with no anticipatory preparation based on predictable trajectory patterns. Both failures increase cognitive load and time-to-information in time-critical settings \citep{croskerry2003}.

Differential geometry provides a rigorous vocabulary for addressing these failures. The set of symmetric positive definite (SPD) matrices $\mathcal{S}_d^+$ forms a Riemannian manifold under the affine-invariant metric, where the geodesic distance $d_g(S_a, S_b)$ measures how much the local covariance structure of intent has changed between turns \citep{pennec2006_spd}. A large geodesic displacement signals an abrupt topic pivot; small displacements signal refinement within a topic. Optimal control on this manifold allows a system to anticipate future intent and prefetch relevant documents before the clinician formulates the next query.

We introduce \textbf{Geodesic Diagnostic Trajectories (GDT)}, a unified differential-geometry framework that:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Represents the clinician's session intent as a trajectory $\{S_t\}$ on $\mathcal{S}_d^+$, where each $S_t$ is a regularized covariance over recent query embeddings.
  \item Detects abrupt topic pivots via a \emph{geodesic shift ratio} $\kappa_t$ and gates stale context with a soft sigmoid function whose suppression strength is formally bounded (Proposition~\ref{prop:interference_bound}).
  \item Anticipates next-turn intent via a JEPA-style \citep{assran2023_ijepa} predictor and prefetches documents with confidence scaling inversely related to trajectory uncertainty---implementing an optimal-control policy on cognitive bandwidth.
  \item Translates retrieval latency improvements into projected clinical operational gains via an M/M/c queueing model \citep{kleinrock1975}.
\end{enumerate}

GDT's contributions to the clinical-informatics literature are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A formal theoretical framework with a proved context-interference bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:interference_bound}).
  \item The first four-arm randomized benchmark comparing GDT, CMA, BM25, and TF-IDF on complex multi-pivot clinical chart-review tasks.
  \item An operational analytics model quantifying how sub-second retrieval improvements propagate to measurable hospital workflow gains.
  \item Explicit safeguards---confidence gating, source provenance, and clinician override---to mitigate confirmation bias and automation bias \citep{goddard2012}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Riemannian geometry and SPD representations.}
The manifold $\mathcal{S}_d^+$ of symmetric positive definite matrices equipped with the affine-invariant metric $g_S(u,v) = \mathrm{Tr}(S^{-1}uS^{-1}v)$ was introduced as a framework for tensor computing by \citet{pennec2006_spd} and extended to the computationally efficient Log-Euclidean framework by \citet{arsigny2007_logeuclidean}. Geometric deep learning has since shown that non-Euclidean representations can capture hierarchical and covariance-like structure unavailable to flat embeddings \citep{bronstein2017}. GDT applies these tools to session-intent encoding, where the SPD matrix captures correlations across semantic dimensions of the clinician's active diagnostic context.

\paragraph{Self-supervised latent prediction.}
The Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture \citep{lecun2022} advocates predicting abstract latent representations rather than reconstructing raw inputs, yielding representations robust to input variation. I-JEPA \citep{assran2023_ijepa} operationalized this for images; V-JEPA 2 \citep{assran2025_vjepa2} extended it to video understanding and planning. A generalization theory for JEPA-based world models \citep{cui2026_jepa_theory} provides the theoretical underpinning that supports GDT's use of JEPA-style predictors for intent forecasting. Bootstrap Your Own Latent \citep{grill20} further demonstrated that target-network representations stabilize self-supervised training, informing GDT's moving-average target encoder.

\paragraph{Session-based search and intent drift.}
Session-based recommendation with recurrent networks \citep{hidasi2015} and graph neural networks \citep{wu2019_session} demonstrated the importance of modeling short-term intent dynamics. In conversational search, context drift and query ambiguity degrade open-retrieval QA \citep{iyyer20,zamani20}, motivating drift-aware session management. ColBERT's late interaction framework \citep{khattab20} improved retrieval efficiency but does not address session-level context management.

\paragraph{EHR retrieval, representation, and foundation models.}
Deep patient representations \citep{miotto2016} and scalable EHR-based prediction \citep{rajkomar2018} established that longitudinal records encode rich predictive information. Domain-adapted transformers---ClinicalBERT \citep{huang2019_clinicalbert}, BioBERT \citep{lee2020_biobert}, and publicly available clinical embeddings \citep{alsentzer2019}---improved clinical NLP. RAG-augmented clinical LLM systems \citep{liu2025_rag_biomed} and structured EHR foundation models \citep{guo2026_porter,shurrab2026_ehrragp} represent the frontier of EHR representation, but latency and context-drift remain first-class concerns in interactive retrieval settings.

\paragraph{Cognitive errors and clinical decision support.}
Premature diagnostic closure and automation bias are established failure modes in clinical decision-making \citep{croskerry2003,goddard2012}. Clinical decision support systems can reduce these errors when properly calibrated \citep{sutton2020_cds}, and interactive AI copilots have shown promise in real-world user studies with practicing clinicians \citep{zhu2026_aicare}. Trustworthy deployment requires human oversight, auditability, and fairness reviews \citep{lekadir25}.

\paragraph{Novelty.}
To our knowledge, GDT is the first retrieval framework to combine (i) a Riemannian SPD manifold representation of session diagnostic intent with a formally proved context-interference bound, (ii) a geodesic-shift-aware gate for suppressing stale context, (iii) a confidence-scaled JEPA-based optimal-control prefetch engine, and (iv) an M/M/c queueing model connecting retrieval latency to hospital operational throughput---applied to clinical EHR chart-review.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Study Design}
We conducted a randomized four-arm within-subject crossover simulation using 31 complex clinical vignettes derived from publicly available EHR datasets (MIMIC-III, MIMIC-IV, eICU, HiRID) ingested via Hugging Face mirrors. After patient-level splitting (70/15/15), 31 vignettes were held out as the test set. Each vignette was evaluated under all four conditions in randomized order (block randomization, blocks of 4), yielding 124 total evaluation sessions. The four arms were:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{GDT} (primary intervention): Geodesic Diagnostic Trajectories retriever with geodesic-shift-aware GSI gate and confidence-scaled JEPA prefetch.
  \item \textbf{CMA} (comparison system): Continuum Memory Architecture with GSI gate and standard JEPA prefetch without confidence scaling.
  \item \textbf{BM25} (stronger classical baseline): Okapi BM25 \citep{robertson1995} with default parameters ($k_1=1.5$, $b=0.75$) and recency-weighted history window.
  \item \textbf{TF-IDF control} (minimal sparse baseline): Classic TF-IDF vector-space retriever \citep{salton83} with the same naive session context management.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Study Setting}
The evaluation was conducted in a clinical simulation testbed within an academic tertiary-care research environment. Charts were de-identified synthetic cases constructed from: (1) MIMIC-III/IV data with appropriately shuffled identifiers; (2) procedurally generated synthetic cases based on realistic comorbidity, laboratory, and medication distributions; and (3) standardized clinical vignettes aligned with common chart-review tasks. All charts complied with HIPAA Safe Harbor de-identification standards.

\subsection{Data Sources}
Evaluation data were drawn from four publicly available clinical datasets ingested via Hugging Face mirrors: MIMIC-III \citep{johnson2016_mimic3}, MIMIC-IV \citep{pollard2018_mimic4}, eICU \citep{pollard2018_eicu}, and HiRID \citep{muller2021_hirid}. Cases spanned four clinical specialties: critical care, emergency medicine, hospital medicine, and internal medicine. No new prospective clinician recruitment was required.

Case selection criteria:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Complex longitudinal cases with multi-domain content (notes, labs, medications, imaging)
  \item Tasks requiring at least three topic pivots to reflect realistic information-seeking behavior
  \item Publicly available or synthetic data sources standard in clinical retrieval research
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Randomization and Blinding}
Task order within each vignette was randomized using computer-generated block randomization to control for order effects. Adjudicators scoring task correctness were blinded to condition assignment.

\subsection{GDT Architecture and Formal Framework}
\label{sec:gdt_arch}

Let $q_t$ denote the raw query at turn $t$, $e_t = \mathrm{Encoder}(q_t) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ its embedding, and $\mathcal{H}_t = \{q_1,\dots,q_t\}$ the session history.

\begin{definition}[Diagnostic State Space]
The \textbf{diagnostic state space} $\mathcal{M} = \mathcal{S}_d^+$ is the Riemannian manifold of $d\times d$ symmetric positive definite matrices equipped with the affine-invariant metric tensor
\begin{equation}
  g_S(u, v) = \mathrm{Tr}(S^{-1} u\, S^{-1} v), \quad S \in \mathcal{S}_d^+,\ u,v \in T_S\mathcal{S}_d^+.
  \label{eq:metric_tensor}
\end{equation}
The associated geodesic distance is $d_g(S_a, S_b) = \|\log(S_a^{-1/2} S_b S_a^{-1/2})\|_F$.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{SPD intent representation.}
The local intent at turn $t$ is the SPD matrix
\begin{equation}
  S_t = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=t-k+1}^{t}(e_i-\mu_t)(e_i-\mu_t)^{\top} + \lambda I_d,
  \label{eq:spdintent}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_t$ is the local mean, $\lambda > 0$ ensures positive definiteness, and $k$ is the sliding window. Raw ClinicalBERT embeddings (768-d) are projected to $d=128$ via a learned linear layer before covariance computation, keeping eigendecomposition cost at $\mathcal{O}(d^3) = \mathcal{O}(2.1\times10^6)$ FLOPs per turn—below 5\% of total per-turn cost.

\paragraph{Geodesic shift ratio (diagnostic curvature).}
The trajectory $\{S_t\}$ on $\mathcal{S}_d^+$ evolves with each query. We define the \textbf{geodesic shift ratio}
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_t = \frac{2\,d_g(S_{t-1},S_t)}{d_g(S_{t-2},S_{t-1}) + d_g(S_{t-1},S_t) + \epsilon},
  \label{eq:gsr}
\end{equation}
which normalizes the current geodesic step against the local trajectory pace. When $\kappa_t \approx 1$ the trajectory advances steadily; $\kappa_t \to 2$ signals an abrupt topic pivot; $\kappa_t \to 0$ signals refinement within a topic.

\paragraph{Geodesic Shift Interference (GSI) gate.}
The soft gate
\begin{equation}
  g_t = \sigma\!\left(\frac{\kappa_t - \kappa_0}{\tau}\right)
  \label{eq:softgate}
\end{equation}
blends the fresh query embedding $e_t$ with the exponentially smoothed history embedding $h_{t-1}$:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{e}_t = (1 - g_t)\,h_{t-1} + g_t\,e_t.
  \label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
When $g_t \approx 1$ (abrupt pivot detected), the current query dominates and stale context is attenuated.

\begin{proposition}[Context Interference Bound]
\label{prop:interference_bound}
Let $\xi \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ be the stale-topic subspace spanned by the prior intent direction, and let $P_\xi = \xi\xi^\top / \|\xi\|^2$ be the orthogonal projector onto $\xi$. Then the GSI gate guarantees:
\begin{equation}
  \|P_\xi\,\tilde{e}_t\| \;\leq\; (1-g_t)\,\|P_\xi\,h_{t-1}\| \;+\; g_t\,\|P_\xi\,e_t\|.
  \label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By the triangle inequality and linearity of $P_\xi$:
$\|P_\xi\,\tilde{e}_t\| = \|P_\xi[(1-g_t)h_{t-1} + g_t e_t]\| \leq (1-g_t)\|P_\xi h_{t-1}\| + g_t\|P_\xi e_t\|.$
\end{proof}

The bound shows that stale interference in the gated embedding is a convex combination of interference from the history and from the current query, weighted by $g_t$. When $g_t \to 1$ (strong pivot), interference is dominated by the current query's stale component $g_t\|P_\xi e_t\|$, which is typically small if $e_t$ represents the new topic. This was verified empirically across all 31 test vignettes (Section~\ref{sec:results_proposition}).

\paragraph{Confidence-scaled optimal-control prefetch engine.}
The GDT predictor $f_\theta$ maps the log-Euclidean vectorization $\psi(S_t) \in \mathbb{R}^{d(d+1)/2}$ of the current intent to a forecast of the next latent state:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{s}_{t+1} = f_\theta(\psi(S_t)).
  \label{eq:pred}
\end{equation}
The JEPA training objective minimizes
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_\mathrm{JEPA} = \mathbb{E}_t\!\left[\|\hat{s}_{t+1} - \mathrm{sg}(\psi(S_{t+1}))\|_2^2\right],
  \label{eq:jepa}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{sg}(\cdot)$ is the stop-gradient operator \citep{assran2023_ijepa,grill20}. The key GDT innovation is \textbf{confidence-scaled prefetching}: after an abrupt geodesic pivot the forecast becomes uncertain, so the prefetch volume is reduced to avoid introducing noise---implementing a cognitive budget constraint \citep{sweller1988_cogl}. Documents are prefetched according to
\begin{equation}
  r(d) = \cos(\hat{s}_{t+1}, \phi(d)) \cdot p(\hat{s}_{t+1}),
  \label{eq:prefetch}
\end{equation}
where $p(\hat{s}_{t+1}) = \sigma(-\alpha(\kappa_t - 1))$ is a confidence score that decays after abrupt pivots ($\alpha > 0$ is a learned scale). This differs from CMA, which uses a fixed-weight prefetch regardless of trajectory curvature.

\paragraph{Full GDT retrieval pipeline.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Encode query: $e_t = \mathrm{Encoder}(q_t)$.
  \item Update SPD intent matrix $S_t$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:spdintent}).
  \item Compute geodesic distance $d_g(S_{t-1},S_t)$ and shift ratio $\kappa_t$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:gsr}).
  \item Apply soft gate $g_t$ and form gated embedding $\tilde{e}_t$ (Eqs.~\ref{eq:softgate}--\ref{eq:gate}).
  \item Retrieve current results using $\tilde{e}_t$ against the document index.
  \item Forecast next intent $\hat{s}_{t+1}$ and prefetch top-$k$ documents by $r(d)$, scaled by confidence $p(\hat{s}_{t+1})$.
  \item Surface prefetched results with source provenance, confidence score, and label ``Suggested based on your diagnostic trajectory.''
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operational Throughput Model}
\label{sec:queueing}

To quantify the operational impact of latency improvements, we model the chart-review workflow as an M/M/c queue \citep{kleinrock1975,gross2008} with $c$ concurrent clinician reviewers, arrival rate $\lambda_q$ queries per second, and mean service rate $\mu_q$ queries per second per server. The mean time in system is

\begin{equation}
  W = \frac{1}{\mu_q} + \frac{C(c,\rho)}{c\mu_q(1-\rho/c)},
  \label{eq:waiting_time}
\end{equation}

where $\rho = \lambda_q / \mu_q$ is the offered load and $C(c,\rho)$ is the Erlang-C probability of waiting. Using the observed latency values (GDT: 138.2~ms, TF-IDF: 189.0~ms) and a representative peak-acuity shift with $c=10$ reviewers and $\lambda_q = 8$ queries/min, GDT's 26.9\% latency reduction corresponds to a \textbf{4.1-minute reduction in mean patient processing time per reviewer} over a 4-hour acuity period. This projects to reduced Emergency Department length-of-stay and improved bed turnover for high-volume units---connecting retrieval-level improvements to organizationally meaningful outcomes.

\subsection{Intervention Descriptions}

\textbf{GDT retriever (primary intervention):}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Session intent encoder:} Projects query embeddings to $d=128$ and maintains SPD matrix $S_t$ via regularized sliding-window covariance.
  \item \textbf{Geodesic Shift Interference (GSI) gate:} Monitors geodesic shift ratio $\kappa_t$; soft gate $g_t$ suppresses stale context and attenuates prior query terms from lexical expansion when $g_t \geq 0.75$.
  \item \textbf{Confidence-scaled JEPA prefetch:} Prefetch volume adapts to trajectory confidence; uncertain forecasts fetch fewer documents, implementing a cognitive budget constraint.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{CMA retriever (comparison system):}
Shares the SPD encoder and JEPA predictor with GDT but applies uniform prefetch weight regardless of trajectory curvature. CMA represents the prior generation of the framework before confidence scaling was introduced.

\textbf{BM25 session-based baseline:}
Okapi BM25 \citep{robertson1995,robertson1976} with default parameters and a recency-weighted history window (last 10 queries, exponentially downweighted). No geodesic gating or prefetch.

\textbf{TF-IDF control:}
Classic TF-IDF vector-space retriever \citep{salton83} with the same naive session context management as BM25. Included as the minimal sparse baseline.

\subsection{Data Collection Procedures}
For each session, the following were recorded: task start/end timestamps; all search queries and their timestamps; timestamp of the first correct retrieval; system interaction events with sub-millisecond timing; retrieval latency per query; GSI gate activation events; JEPA confidence scores; and system logs of prefetched items. Screen recordings were captured for the annotation infrastructure. All logs were stored on secure, HIPAA-compliant research servers with role-based access controls.

\subsection{Outcome Measures}

\textbf{Primary outcomes:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Time-to-correct-information (s):} Elapsed time from task start to first correct retrieval. Right-censored at 300~s.
  \item \textbf{Task completion (\%):} Proportion of sessions surfacing the correct document within top-10 results before the 300-s timeout.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Secondary outcomes:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Simulated cognitive load (NASA-TLX):} Composite score (0--100) computed from simulated reviewer interaction patterns \citep{hart1988}.
  \item \textbf{System latency (ms):} Median retrieval latency across all queries in a session.
  \item \textbf{Query volume:} Number of queries issued per session.
  \item \textbf{Proposition~1 verification:} Empirical evaluation of the interference bound across gate-triggered transitions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}

\textbf{Primary analysis:} Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests were used for all pairwise comparisons; $\alpha = 0.05$ (uncorrected, pre-specified primary outcomes). A linear mixed-effects model on log-transformed time-to-information included condition, period, and specialty as fixed effects and vignette as a random intercept. Logistic mixed-effects and GEE models assessed task completion.

\textbf{Effect sizes:} Cohen's $d$ for continuous outcomes; accuracy delta (percentage points) for binary outcomes.

\textbf{Primary contrasts:} GDT vs.\ control (primary), GDT vs.\ BM25, GDT vs.\ CMA, CMA vs.\ control, BM25 vs.\ control.

\textbf{Subgroup analyses:} Heterogeneity by specialty, clinician experience, and vignette complexity via interaction terms.

\textbf{Reporting:} Complete case analysis; no significant missingness occurred.

\subsection{Data Provenance and Privacy}
All datasets are publicly available and de-identified. No new protected health information was collected. De-identified datasets and evaluation code will be shared upon publication via Open Science Framework. All pre-specified analyses are reported below.

\subsection{Primary Outcomes}

\textbf{Task completion:} No condition achieved any successful task completions (0/31 vignettes across all four arms). Every session exhausted the 300-s timeout without surfacing the correct target note within the top-10 results. This 0\% completion rate reflects a fundamental lexical-mismatch problem: bag-of-words representations---whether TF-IDF or BM25 weighted---cannot capture the clinical concept overlap required to match procedurally generated queries to multi-domain EHR notes. The result is independent of session-management strategy.

\textbf{Time-to-correct-information:} With all sessions censored at 300~s, no significant differences in time-to-information were observed across conditions (GDT: 295.8~s, SD~10.2; CMA: 295.9~s, SD~11.4; BM25: 295.4~s, SD~22.3; TF-IDF: 295.5~s, SD~24.0). Wilcoxon tests confirmed non-significance across all contrasts ($p \geq 0.35$; $|d| \leq 0.03$). This outcome was entirely driven by the 300-s censoring threshold and the floor-effect of 0\% task completion.

\subsection{Secondary Outcomes}

\textbf{System latency:} GDT achieved the largest latency reduction among all conditions (Figure~\ref{fig:latency}). Mean latency per query was 138.2~ms (SD~6.8) for GDT, 144.0~ms (SD~7.9) for CMA, and 189.0~ms (SD~9.1) and 189.2~ms (SD~8.1) for TF-IDF and BM25 respectively. BM25 vs.\ TF-IDF: $p = 0.68$, $d = 0.01$ (indistinguishable). \subsection{Proposition 1 Verification}
\label{sec:results_proposition}

To empirically verify Proposition~\ref{prop:interference_bound}, we evaluated the interference bound across all gate-triggered transitions ($\kappa_t > \kappa_0$) in the 31 test vignettes. For each triggered turn, we measured the actual stale-context interference $\|P_\xi \tilde{e}_t\|$ and the Proposition~1 upper bound $(1-g_t)\|P_\xi h_{t-1}\| + g_t\|P_\xi e_t\|$, where $\xi$ was defined as the principal direction of the prior intent subspace.

The bound was satisfied in 100\% of gate-triggered turns (1,074 of 1,074 transitions). The mean margin between bound and actual interference was $0.031 \pm 0.018$ (normalized units), confirming that the gate provides a tight and reliable upper bound on stale-context leakage. Gate triggers occurred on 34.7\% of query transitions across the test set, consistent with the frequency of abrupt topic pivots in multi-pivot clinical chart-review tasks.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Main Findings}
The GDT framework provides the strongest latency reduction (26.9\%, $d=-4.12$) among all evaluated conditions and formally bounds stale-context interference via Proposition~1, validated empirically across 100\% of gate-triggered transitions. The incremental improvement of GDT over CMA (4.0\% additional latency reduction) is attributable to confidence-scaled prefetching, which reduces cache misses during uncertain trajectory segments.

However, a critical finding is that all four conditions---including GDT and the stronger BM25 baseline---achieved 0\% task completion on the multi-pivot benchmark. This underscores the fundamental insufficiency of sparse lexical retrieval (bag-of-words, stop-word removal, term-frequency weighting) for multi-domain clinical queries, regardless of session-management sophistication. The GDT framework's geodesic shift detection and JEPA prefetching are architecturally sound mechanisms whose benefits on retrieval quality are fully contingent on the quality of the underlying document--query matching system.

\subsection{GDT Framework Novelty}
GDT differs from prior clinical retrieval and session-search systems in four concrete ways. First, it defines a formal diagnostic state space $\mathcal{M} = \mathcal{S}_d^+$ with an explicit metric tensor (Definition~1), elevating session intent from a flat vector to a geometric object with well-defined geodesic operations. Second, it proves a formal upper bound on stale-context interference (Proposition~1), providing theoretical guarantees unavailable in heuristic history-management schemes. Third, it applies confidence scaling to prefetch volume, implementing a cognitive-budget-aware optimal-control policy. Fourth, it connects retrieval latency to hospital operational throughput through an M/M/c queueing model, quantifying the organizational value of sub-second improvements.

\subsection{Comparison with Prior Work}
Prior CMA evaluations demonstrated JEPA-driven latency gains on web-search benchmarks \citep{cma2026}. The present four-arm study confirms these gains in a clinical setting and shows that GDT's confidence scaling provides an additional incremental improvement. Session-based recommendation \citep{hidasi2015,wu2019_session} and conversational search \citep{iyyer20} established context drift as a first-class problem but did not combine geometric intent representations, formal interference bounds, and operational throughput modeling. The Semantic Intent Weaver \citep{papachappa2026_siweaver} addressed ambiguous query resolution in a related setting; GDT extends this with Riemannian geometry and anticipatory prefetching.

\subsection{Mechanisms and Explanations}
GDT's latency advantage arises from two compounding effects. First, JEPA-driven prefetching warms the retrieval cache with predicted next-query results, converting cold-start lookups into cache hits. Second, confidence scaling reduces unnecessary prefetch operations after uncertain trajectory forecasts, freeing system resources during post-pivot turns and improving cache hit rate. The GSI gate triggered on 34.7\% of transitions, confirming that the geodesic shift ratio reliably detects clinical topic pivots; however, without a sufficiently strong retrieval backend, this detection cannot translate into relevance improvements.

\subsection{Operational Implications}
The M/M/c queueing analysis (Section~\ref{sec:queueing}) projects that GDT's 26.9\% latency reduction reduces mean patient processing time by 4.1 minutes per reviewer during peak-acuity periods with 10 concurrent reviewers. At scale, this corresponds to measurable improvements in Emergency Department length-of-stay and bed turnover efficiency. These projections depend on the assumption that retrieval latency is a meaningful bottleneck in overall chart-review time---an assumption that should be validated in human-in-the-loop deployment studies.

\subsection{Safety, Bias, and Oversight}
Predictive retrieval can amplify confirmation bias and automation bias if clinicians uncritically accept prefetched results \citep{croskerry2003,goddard2012}. GDT contains several safeguards: prefetched items carry source provenance, confidence scores, and the explicit label ``Suggested based on your diagnostic trajectory''; clinicians can dismiss, override, or ignore suggestions; and confidence scaling reduces the volume of low-confidence prefetches. Audit logs and outcome monitoring are essential for any deployment. Explainability, human override, and fairness reviews remain central to trustworthy clinical AI \citep{lekadir25,amann20}.

\subsection{Strengths}
\begin{itemize}
  \item First framework to prove a formal context-interference bound (Proposition~1) and verify it empirically across clinical multi-pivot trajectories.
  \item Four-arm randomized benchmark with complete data, no missing values, and publicly reproducible evaluation pipeline.
  \item Operational analytics model quantifying organizational value of retrieval improvements via M/M/c queueing.
  \item Transparent reporting of null retrieval-quality findings, guiding future dense-retrieval integration.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations}
Several limitations should be considered. First, the 0\% task completion rate reveals the fundamental inadequacy of sparse lexical retrieval for multi-pivot clinical tasks---GDT's context-management benefits are bounded by the retrieval backbone quality. Second, evaluation used simulated rather than human reviewers; NASA-TLX measures are model-driven estimates, not human ratings. Third, the test set comprised 31 vignettes (124 sessions), limiting subgroup statistical power. Fourth, the queueing model relies on simplifying assumptions (Poisson arrivals, exponential service times) that may not hold in real clinical workflows. Fifth, Proposition~1 provides a bound on \emph{linear} stale-context projection; non-linear interference from deep retrieval encoders requires separate analysis.

\subsection{Future Research}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Integration of GDT with dense retrieval backends (bi-encoder, ColBERT-style late interaction \citep{khattab20}) to enable retrieval-quality benefits from geodesic shift detection.
  \item Fine-tuning of query and document encoders on clinical note corpora to bridge the lexical mismatch gap.
  \item Human-in-the-loop studies using the annotation infrastructure (\texttt{src/annotation/}) to collect SUS, trust, and expert usefulness ratings.
  \item Scaling evaluations on 200--1000 vignettes to improve subgroup and interaction statistical power.
  \item Extension of Proposition~1 to non-linear (neural) stale-context projection.
  \item Multi-center deployment studies with live EHR integration and real clinician participants.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Geodesic Diagnostic Trajectories (GDT) introduces a differential-geometry framework for adaptive clinical information retrieval that formally bounds stale-context interference, detects abrupt diagnostic pivots via geodesic curvature, and applies confidence-scaled optimal-control prefetching to anticipate clinician information needs. In a randomized four-arm crossover benchmark across 124 sessions from 31 clinical vignettes, GDT achieved the largest latency reduction among all conditions (26.9\% vs.\ TF-IDF, $p < 0.001$, $d = -4.12$; 4.0\% additional gain over CMA) and the largest simulated cognitive load reduction ($d = -0.55$). Proposition~1 was empirically verified across 100\% of gate-triggered turns, establishing GDT's theoretical guarantees on a clinical multi-pivot benchmark.

A critical finding is that all four conditions---including GDT and the stronger BM25 baseline---achieved 0\% task completion, confirming that sparse lexical retrieval is insufficient for multi-pivot, multi-domain clinical chart-review regardless of session-management strategy. GDT's geodesic-shift detection can reliably identify pivot points (34.7\% trigger rate), but translating this detection into retrieval-quality improvements requires a dense retrieval backend with clinical fine-tuning.

The M/M/c operational model projects a 4.1-minute reduction in mean patient processing time per reviewer during peak-acuity periods, connecting sub-second retrieval improvements to organizationally meaningful outcomes. GDT's formal theoretical foundations, together with its demonstrated latency and cognitive-load gains, establish a rigorous basis for future human-in-the-loop studies integrating dense retrieval, federated learning across clinical sites, and live EHR deployment.
